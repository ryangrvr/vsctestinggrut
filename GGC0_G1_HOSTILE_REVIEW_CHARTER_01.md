## 4. Required attacks on every atomic item

Review all sixteen A-n entries, not only the G2-eligible eight.

### H1 — Scope attack

Ask whether the original answer classified the item at the scope actually
licensed by the record. Distinguish: fundamental theory; effective theory;
model within a theory; parameter within a model; state/history/boundary
condition; analyst or experimental interface choice. "Required to specify
this effective model" is not automatically interchangeable with "fundamental
law of reality." The frozen criterion still governs; this attack tests
whether the original reasoning applied it at the correct scope.

### H2 — Same-rule witness attack

Search the accepted record for a witness consisting of two admissible
instances governed by one unchanged specification but differing in the item.
If such a witness exists, a LAW-LIKE Q1/Q2 answer must confront it explicitly.
Merely observing that different choices give different phenomenology does not
establish LAW-LIKE status.

### H3 — Model-versus-law attack

For every original LAW-LIKE component, ask whether the purported "law" is
instead a model-defining choice inside a broader unchanged framework.
Especially hostile treatment for: generator versus state under a fixed
dynamical framework; nonlinear drift/potential versus the framework admitting
potentials; access/readout versus physical law; lift versus
representation/model realization; coupling parameters versus interaction law.
Do not change the criterion; determine whether the original answer actually
satisfies it.

### H4 — Bundling attack

Where an atomic Ledger item contains heterogeneous subcomponents, ask whether
those subcomponents behave differently under Q1 or Q2. The hostile review may
**not split the frozen A-n unit**. If one bundled component supports LAW-LIKE
while another supports SOLUTION-LIKE or cannot vary independently, and the
item cannot coherently receive one answer as a whole, the operative answer is
INDETERMINATE. Mandatory for A-8, A-14, A-16, and any item whose original
reasoning identifies internal heterogeneity.

### H5 — Dependence/double-counting attack

Ask whether the item is genuinely independent at the frozen atomic grain or
merely a price/subcomponent of another residual item. If varying it
necessarily changes another A-n item, Q1 must respect the frozen
non-independence rule and cannot infer LAW-LIKE merely from that dependence.
A-2, A-4 and A-10 require explicit treatment.

### H6 — Criterion-circularity attack

An answer may not establish LAW-LIKE merely by matching the item's noun to
one of the examples named in Q2. The review must identify why, in the
accepted record and at the relevant scope, the item actually determines the
permissible-solution structure rather than merely selecting one admitted
model or interface.

### H7 — Counterargument effectiveness attack

The original adversarial counterargument must be evaluated, not merely noted.
For each entry state: whether the counterargument would actually flip Q1 or
Q2 if accepted; whether the original classification supplied a reason for
rejecting it; whether that rejection is grounded in accepted evidence. A
counterargument exposing an unresolved criterion conflict that was never
answered is grounds for demotion to INDETERMINATE.
