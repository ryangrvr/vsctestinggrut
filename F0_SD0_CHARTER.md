**attempt an actual generative principle** — or to prove that such a principle cannot

## 2. Central question

**Can a compact invariant principle generate the admissible support family itself?**
Target object (input §2; CONSTRUCTED terminology): the admissible support family
𝔖_T(R) = { Supp(p_s) : s ∈ States_T(R) }, indexed by a declared physical/task
realization R; envelope P_T(R) = ⋃ S; the individual state support S_s is state-level;
P_T(R) \ S_s (possible-but-zero-weight) is a **consequence to check**, not the first
law target. The scenario-level spectrum is a secondary comparator object only.

First serious discriminator: **PR₍₂ₓ₂₎ forbidden while quantum strong contextuality at
3×3 is allowed — and capacity counting may not explain the difference by fiat.**

## 3. Frozen firewalls

1. **R1–R15 binding** at the governing map; every supplied input priced (R10); the
   F0-PHYS gate is OPEN, so all possibility talk is **declared**-possibility (R11);
   representation invariance (R1) over the declared equivalence moves only.
2. **Prohibited inputs:** Hilbert orthogonality / inner-product structure, GPT cones,
   exclusivity axioms, quantum-lattice (orthomodular/Piron/Solèr) axioms, operational
   reconstruction axioms. These exist in this campaign **only as null comparators**
   (§7).
3. **No lookup tables:** scenario names, party counts, setting counts, and Hilbert
   dimensions may not appear as case-splits, parameters, or side conditions of a
   candidate law. "Dimension" is not a fundamental variable GRUT is allowed to see.
4. **Possibility/probability firewall:** all SD0 gates are support-level (Boolean
   patterns). Probability inequalities (Local Orthogonality included) are comparators,
   never inputs; crossing from possibility into weights anywhere in a candidate law's
   statement is a charter violation.
5. **Out of scope:** any weights/measure law, the Born rule, outcome selection, F0-B
   (access-law dynamics `R_Γ`/`A_Γ`/`Cl_Γ`/`S`/`U`), physicality claims about `C`.
6. **Candidate preregistration:** a candidate principle must be committed as an exact,
   finite-checkable rule **before** it is evaluated on the controls; post-hoc
   modification of an evaluated candidate is a new candidate.
