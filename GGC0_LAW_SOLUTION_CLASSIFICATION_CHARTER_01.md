## 1. Purpose

Determine what a GGC0 generative law would be *obligated* to explain, before
inventing any such law. This charter classifies each surviving supplied input
(designated below as "the Ledger") into one of three bins:

- **LAW-LIKE**
- **SOLUTION-LIKE**
- **MIXED / UNRESOLVED**

The bins are defined in §3. The Ledger is identified in §5. The classification
itself is NOT part of this document.

## 3. The classification criterion (the only operative content of this charter)

For each Ledger item I, ask exactly three questions, in order. The answers,
not intuition, fix the bin.

**Q1 (Variation test).** If I were varied arbitrarily — keeping every other
Ledger item and every earned constraint fixed — would the resulting object be
(a) a different solution of the *same* theory, or (b) a different *theory*?
Bin (b) answers push toward LAW-LIKE; bin (a) answers push toward
SOLUTION-LIKE.

**Q2 (Counterfactual-observer test).** Could two observers with access to the
same earned structures disagree about I and still be describing the same
world? If disagreement over I changes which structures are earned at all
(e.g., it changes what counts as a subsystem, a measurement, or a law),
I is LAW-LIKE. If disagreement over I corresponds to two histories of one
world, I is SOLUTION-LIKE.

**Q3 (Precedent test).** Does an existing accepted physical theory at
comparable scope treat analogous information as law or as state?
- Analogy counts only at matched scope: e.g., GR treats the metric's
  *equation* as law and the *particular metric* as solution data; QFT treats
  the action's operator content as law and coupling values as inputs whose
  status is genuinely contested (hence MIXED is available, not a failure).
- Precedent may support but never overrule Q1/Q2: if Q1 and Q2 both say
  LAW-LIKE and precedent says otherwise, the item is recorded LAW-LIKE with
  the precedent conflict noted; the converse also holds.

**Bin rules.**
- Both Q1 and Q2 → LAW-LIKE: **LAW-LIKE** (Q3 conflict noted).
- Both → SOLUTION-LIKE: **SOLUTION-LIKE** (Q3 conflict noted).
- Q1 and Q2 disagree, or either answer is not defensible from the record:
  **MIXED / UNRESOLVED**. No tie-breaking judgment is permitted in execution.
  MIXED is a real bin and may be a final classification.

**Explicit prohibitions during classification.**
- No item may be moved to SOLUTION-LIKE because moving it would help GGC0
  (or to LAW-LIKE because it would help a candidate law). Motive-blindness
  is the point of freezing this criterion.
- No re-grading of the underlying earned results; the Ledger items enter with
  their recorded statuses.
- No merging of items that the Ledger records as distinct, and no splitting
  of items the record treats as one — the unit of classification is the
  record's own unit.
- Every classification must cite the source record for the item's current
  status AND give one adversarial counterargument to the assigned bin. A
  classification without a recorded counterargument is invalid.
