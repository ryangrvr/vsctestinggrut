## 0. Top findings

1. **Explicit text lets FDT and Onsager in.** STD-3 fires only "under RH-KMS with RH-HAM". STD-4 needs RH-KMS. STD-6 needs RH-HAM "with Π supplied". NR-12(b) can be read as saying that a premise 𝒦 *generates* is not standard. Together these let a stochastic detailed-balance dynamics, or a GKSL one, pass Q3 with FDT, regression or Onsager relations. CV-1 cannot override this, because the text is explicit rather than ambiguous (RT-S-01, RT-S-02).
2. **The ΔF formula credits each axis separately**, through `ΔF(ι) = p·c(ι) + min(16, ΔF_Π) + min(log₂5, ΔF_T) + ΔF_supp`. That contradicts Addition 2 ("jointly realized … beyond") and FC-3 (D_diff = 0). Differentiation bits and the choice of a standard model class leak into lock credit once Q5 is passed with a single rectangle (RT-S-07).
3. **The baseline is a union, the null is a lookup, and the regime list is closed.** Freedom is measured against the union of all standard models. §4.4 compares against a null that pays lookup price for Γ. 𝒮 is closed over 13 regime hypotheses. So "the law lands in a known standard class" scores as freedom reduction and can be banked as a COMPRESSIVE sector through OR-5 (RT-S-04, RT-S-05, RT-S-06).
4. **The charter's own design steers cards into standard territory.** The frozen protocol templates (0, ±α, 2α) invite Volterra interpolation. The mandatory symmetric probe (NR-5) leads to symmetry breaking, where Curie selection rules relative to the generated Π apply (RT-S-12, RT-S-16).
5. **A mathematical error.** "dim_ub … by an exact rank computation at an explicit point" gives a *lower* bound on dimension, not an upper one. Choosing a symmetric or equilibrium point inflates codimension (RT-S-22).

Residual defense: most patterns below are caught, if at all, only at S9 by the comparator panel's recall. A card that reaches S9 having passed S6 is still bankable as COMPRESSIVE through CARD-ASSEMBLY (OR-5). So standard physics can earn a scoreboard status even when it is never picked.

## 1. Gaming patterns

### RT-S-01 [CRITICAL] Laundering a generated premise (Gibbs, detailed balance or KMS generated, then FDT credited)
- **Pattern (CHARTER TEST).**
  - The law's solutions have a reference state that is detailed-balanced, Gibbs-like or KMS. This is generated, with a W-pipe: some non-solutions lack it.
  - The premise itself is booked as RECOVERY, worth 0.
  - Downstream relations derived through that premise are then claimed as law-derived: FDT, Onsager regression, fluctuation-theorem identities, or the second FDT. They are written in (Π, T_Π, ε_R, Γ_Π) variables.
- **Slips through.**
  - NR-12(b): "A Gibbs, KMS, FDT, detailed-balance or Onsager premise that 𝒦 **does not generate** (with a W-pipe) makes the claim STANDARD-IMPLIED." By converse reading, a generated premise does not make the claim STANDARD-IMPLIED.
  - NR-12: "Gibbs or KMS properties of the solutions are outputs. They count as RECOVERY". This zeroes the premise only, not its consequences.
- **Why it violates intent.** G2-11 Addition 3 says relations implied by KMS, FDT or Onsager earn nothing, whatever the source of the premise.
- **Fix (replace NR-12(b)):**
  > "(b) Every premise of each credited derivation is listed. If any premise is a PS-5 structure (invariant/Gibbs/KMS measure, detailed balance, FDT kernel, fluctuation-theorem premise, Onsager symmetry), **whether supplied, hidden or generated by 𝒦**, every claim whose derivation passes through it is STANDARD-IMPLIED and earns 0. Generation changes only the label of the premise itself (SUPPLIED → RECOVERY), never the status of its consequences. **PS-5 substitution test:** replace Sol by standard models with the same PS-5 premise and the same H(x) (B-HB or card-supplied, independently verified). If ℛ★ holds on all of them, it is STANDARD-IMPLIED."

### RT-S-02 [CRITICAL] Hypothesis gates on STD-3, STD-4 and STD-6 exclude non-Hamiltonian equilibrium
- **Pattern (CHARTER TEST).**
  - The realized S+E dynamics is stochastic or GKSL, so it is not closed at record resolution, but it is reversible with respect to its stationary reference.
  - Detailed balance can be automatic, by Kolmogorov's criterion, whenever the generated state graph has no cycles: birth–death chains, two-state blocks, trees.
  - Linear FDT, Onsager reciprocity and the Mori–Zwanzig/GLE decomposition all hold. But RH-HAM is false, and RH-NESS ("non-equilibrium") is false too, so none of the three items fires.
  - Q3 witnesses exist inside the same regime: non-reversible dynamics that keep the same stationary measure (lifted chains) violate the FDT form.
- **Slips through.**
  - STD-3: "Applies under RH-KMS with RH-HAM; the NESS forms need RH-NESS and RH-MK."
  - RH-NESS: "stationary non-equilibrium Markov".
  - STD-4: "Applies under RH-MR with RH-KMS".
  - STD-6: "Applies under RH-HAM with Π supplied."
- **Fix.**
  - Add **RH-DB**: "the realized generator (Hamiltonian, stochastic or GKSL) satisfies classical or quantum detailed balance with respect to its reference state, including detailed balance implied by Kolmogorov's criterion."
  - STD-3 → "Applies under RH-KMS together with any one of RH-HAM, RH-DB or RH-MK. The NESS forms (Agarwal; Seifert–Speck; Harada–Sasa; Baiesi–Maes–Wynants) apply under any stationary Markov reference, equilibrium included."
  - STD-4 → "(a) dynamical reciprocity, S_ij = ε_iε_j S_ji, under RH-MR or RH-DB at any temperature; (b) Onsager–Casimir under (a) plus RH-KMS; (c) nonlinear forms (Andrieux–Gaspard) under the fluctuation-theorem hypotheses."
  - STD-6 → "Applies always. The Mori–Zwanzig/Dyson identity holds for any linear generator and any projection, supplied or generated. The second FDT needs RH-KMS; the Markov limit needs RH-WC."
  - Add self-test ST-6 (§3).

### RT-S-03 [HIGH] Escape through ill-typed regime hypotheses
- **Pattern (CHARTER TEST).**
  - Ξ's declared type has no energy function, Hilbert space or symplectic structure (VB-11 and VB-3 are not bought).
  - The card argues that RH-KMS, RH-HAM, RH-GAU and RH-LIN are *ill-typed*, and therefore false rather than "unresolved", on every instance.

- **Slips through.**
  - BP-4 covers only "status at x is unresolved".
  - §13.2 phrases every hypothesis in Hamiltonian or Gibbs vocabulary ("KMS or Gibbs for the realized generator", "closed generator").
- **Fix.** Add Appendix F, "Operational surrogates". Each RH is decided on the realized process at record level:
  - RH-KMS ⇔ the stationary reference process is time-reversal invariant with declared parities (classical), or satisfies KMS for its own certified time evolution at some auditor-fitted β (quantum);
  - RH-HAM ⇔ the S+E map at record resolution is deterministic, invertible and measure-preserving;
  - RH-GAU ⇔ the a₀ records of E are jointly Gaussian and the response of E to S is affine;
  - and so on for the rest.

  Then add: "A hypothesis that is ill-typed for the declared type is evaluated through its surrogate. If the surrogate is undecidable, BP-4 applies and the hypothesis is taken to hold. Fitting a temperature-like parameter costs the baseline nothing."

### RT-S-04 [HIGH] Closed hypothesis list: consequences of structural classes are missing from 𝒮
- **Pattern (CHARTER TEST).** The realized set lies in a standard structural class whose defining hypothesis is not one of the 13 RHs. ℛ★ is a published theorem of that class. Examples:
  - finite-range generator → light-cone (Lieb–Robinson) bounds;
  - stationarity → Wiener–Khinchin;
  - local detailed balance → fluctuation theorems and TUR;
  - mixing → CLT for dependent fields;
  - extra conserved charges → GGE with generalized FDT;
  - Markov jump networks → graph-theoretic (matrix-tree) response equalities;
  - quantum structure → information–disturbance relations and Brandão–Piani–Horodecki objectivity.

  Q3 witnesses come from outside the class, so the relation passes.
- **Slips through.**
  - §13.4: "𝒮 includes every relation derivable **from STD-1 to STD-12** by published standard theorems".
  - The closed RH table in §13.2.
  - STD-2 lists only "Uncertainty and Tsirelson-type bounds" once quantum structure is present.
- **Fix (replace §13.4):**
  > "𝒮(x) is the set of relations implied, by any published theorem verified against primary sources, by **any** standard hypothesis true at x. The RH table is a floor: the auditor may add any published structural or regime hypothesis with an operational test, and BP-3/BP-4 apply to it. Floor additions: RH-DB, RH-LDB, RH-STAT, RH-LOC (finite-range generator), RH-ERG (mixing), RH-GGE, RH-PERT (§RT-S-12), RH-NEQ (small affinities), RH-MC (finite bath / microcanonical), RH-Q (quantum structure present: every theorem of quantum theory and quantum information enters 𝒮).
